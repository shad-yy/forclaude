## Where to Watch: Official UK Broadcasters

In the UK, the broadcasting rights for the Premier League are divided among three primary corporate entities. To watch every legally televised match, you must hold active subscriptions with all three providers. Here is how the coverage breaks down for the 2026/27 season:

### Sky Sports (£43/month)
Sky Sports remains the dominant force in Premier League broadcasting, holding the rights to the lion's share of the matches. For the 2026/27 season, Sky Sports will broadcast 128 live matches. These are typically spread across Friday Night Football, Saturday evening (5:30 pm), Super Sunday (2:00 pm and 4:30 pm), and Monday Night Football. A standalone Sky Sports subscription currently costs around £43 per month, making it the most expensive component of any football fan's monthly outgoings. 

### TNT Sports (£30.99/month)
TNT Sports (formerly BT Sport) holds the rights to 52 live Premier League matches. Their coverage primarily focuses on the Saturday lunchtime kick-offs (12:30 pm) and occasionally midweek evening fixtures. To access TNT Sports, viewers must subscribe via discovery+ Premium, which currently costs £30.99 per month. 

### Amazon Prime Video (£8.99/month)
Amazon Prime Video changed the landscape by purchasing the rights to 20 live Premier League matches per season. These are strictly confined to two specific matchweeks: one midweek round in December and the traditional Boxing Day festive fixtures. At £8.99 per month, it is the cheapest of the three, but it also offers the fewest matches.

### BBC Sport (Free Highlights)
It is important to state that you do not need to pay a subscription to watch Premier League highlights in the UK. The BBC retains the rights to broadcast Match of the Day on Saturday nights and Match of the Day 2 on Sundays. These programmes are entirely free to watch on terrestrial television or via the BBC iPlayer (assuming you have a standard UK TV licence). However, the BBC does not broadcast any live Premier League matches.

## The 3PM Saturday Blackout Problem

You might have noticed "None (Blackout)" listed several times in the fixture table above. This brings us to the most contentious issue in UK sports broadcasting: the 3pm Saturday blackout.

Introduced in the 1960s, the blackout rule prohibits the live television broadcast of any football match in the UK between 2:45 pm and 5:15 pm on a Saturday. The original rationale was to protect stadium attendances for lower-league clubs. The fear was that if a blockbuster Premier League match was broadcast live at 3pm, fans of local teams would choose to stay home and watch the television rather than brave the weather to support their local sides.

While the landscape of football has evolved drastically over the last sixty years, this rule remains firmly in place. This creates a uniquely frustrating situation where football fans in the United States, Australia, and the Middle East can watch every single Premier League match live on their local networks, while fans in the UK—the home of the league—cannot. 

Even if you pay the exorbitant £83 per month for Sky Sports, TNT Sports, and Amazon Prime, you are still legally prevented from watching your team play if their match happens to fall within the 3pm Saturday window.
